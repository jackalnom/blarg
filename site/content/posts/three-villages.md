author: Lucas Pierce

title: "Three villages"

date: 2026-11-18

description: A thought experiment on the morality of Pareto improvements, fairness, and strategic behavior in repeated negotiations.

---

There are three equally wealthy villages: A, B, and C. None can afford a flour mill on its own. The leaders of all three villages meet to discuss sharing the cost of a joint flour mill, which would cost each village €40 annually if split equally. Villages A and C agree, but B says it cannot afford the cost because it provides more generous pension benefits to its citizens.

Villages A and C establish and operate the flour mill together, each paying €55 annually. A year later, Village B asks to join. Serving B would cost only an additional €10, and B offers to pay €20. This would lower A and C’s annual payments to €50 each. All three villages would benefit from the arrangement. A and C agree to B's offer.

Another year later, the people of C become unhappy that Village B pays less for flour despite being equally wealthy. Before negotiations for the following year’s contract, the people of C pass a law prohibiting their leaders from agreeing to any future contract in which C pays more for flour than another village.

C says it would prefer that B agree to pay its fair share. If B refuses, C is willing to return to the original two-village arrangement, even though doing so would mean paying more and excluding B. Alternatively, A is free to let B pay only $20 so long as it gives the same pricing to C.

B continues to refuse to pay more than €20, arguing that doing so would require lowering its existing pension benefits.

A now faces two choices:

1. **Keep B:** A pays €80, while B and C pay €20 each. The higher cost leaves more of A’s citizens hungry.
2. **Exclude B:** A and C return to paying €55 each. A’s citizens are better off, but B loses access to affordable flour, leaving many of its citizens hungry.

B argues that the original agreement benefited everyone and that its citizens should not suffer because of C’s actions. B deceived no one, and a village should not be punished for negotiating a better deal. Until C’s ultimatum, everyone benefited from the arrangement. B argues that abstract principles of fairness should not take precedence over human suffering. Furthermore, A has a moral obligation to continue supplying B rather than penalize it for another village’s brinkmanship.

C argues that it is unfair for B to pay less when all three villages are equally wealthy. C maintains that its principle is universal: if every village behaved like B, everyone would suffer. The only reason B pays less is that it was less willing to contribute its fair share. Why should B be rewarded for its intransigence? Moreso, C argues that prioritizing fairness establishes stable, long-term relationships and encourages cooperation in future agreements.

**What should A do? Is Village B or Village C acting more morally? Does your answer change if C had insisted on equal contributions when B first asked to join the existing flour mill arrangement? Why?**
